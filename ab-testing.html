Marketing Analytics Case Study

H&M Email Campaign A/B Test

A statistical experiment evaluating whether urgency-based promotional messaging could improve email open rates and strengthen customer engagement.

Excel A/B Testing Hypothesis Testing Statistical Analysis Marketing Analytics

01

Business Question

Can urgency-based wording increase email open rates?

Control Subject Line

“20% off + free shipping”

Test Subject Line

“Your last chance to score 20% off + free shipping”

02

Experiment Design

Two groups with one controlled difference.

The subject line was the only intended difference between the two email versions. The analysis compared whether recipients opened the control or test email.

9,437 Total recipients
4,764 Control recipients
4,673 Test recipients
1,730 Total email opens

03

Hypotheses

Defining the statistical test.

Null Hypothesis

There is no difference in open rates between the control subject line and the test subject line.

Alternative Hypothesis

The urgency-based test subject line produces a different—and expectedly higher—open rate than the control subject line.

04

Sample Planning

Ensuring the experiment was large enough.

Historical Open Rate

H&M promotional emails historically had an open rate of approximately 18%.

Target Open Rate

The business wanted to determine whether the new subject line could increase the open rate to approximately 20%.

Minimum Detectable Effect

The desired increase represented an approximate 11.1% relative improvement over the historical rate.

Required Sample

The workbook estimated that approximately 9,300 to 9,400 total observations were needed. The actual experiment included 9,437.

05

Results

The urgency-based subject line produced the higher open rate.

Control

17.55%

836 opens from 4,764 recipients

Test

19.13%

894 opens from 4,673 recipients

+1.58 Percentage-point improvement
+9.0% Relative lift in open rate
98% Approximate statistical confidence
p < 0.05 Statistically significant result

06

Interpretation

Urgency improved initial campaign engagement.

Statistical Meaning

The probability that the difference occurred only because of random variation was below the 5% significance threshold.

Business Meaning

Adding urgency to the promotion encouraged more recipients to open the email, suggesting that deadline-oriented messaging may improve initial campaign engagement.

07

Potential Confounding Factors

Protecting the validity of the experiment.

Audience Differences

If recipients were not randomly assigned, one group may have contained more highly engaged customers, creating a misleading difference in open rates.

Timing Differences

Sending the emails at different times could affect open rates independently of the subject line.

Recommended controls: Randomly assign recipients, send both versions at the same time, and keep the email content, design, offer, and audience criteria identical.

08

Recommendations

Apply the result while measuring deeper business impact.

Use the Test Subject Line

Continue using the urgency-based wording because it produced the higher and statistically significant open rate.

Track Click-Through Rate

Measure whether the increase in email opens leads to more customers clicking through to the website.

Measure Purchases and Revenue

Confirm whether stronger engagement translates into actual sales and incremental revenue.

Continue Testing

Test future subject lines while controlling timing, audience selection, email design, and promotional offers.
