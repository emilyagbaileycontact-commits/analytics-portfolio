Database Analytics Case Study

Hospital Database and Analytics Project

A relational database and analytics project designed to help a mid-sized outpatient hospital improve visibility into patient activity, appointments, provider workload, diagnoses, billing, insurance, and operational performance.

SQL PostgreSQL DBeaver Google Cloud Platform Tableau

01

Business Problem

Increasing patient volume created operational challenges.

The Challenge

As patient volume increased, the hospital faced challenges with appointment management, provider workload distribution, billing transparency, diagnosis tracking, and operational reporting.

The Objective

Create a scalable relational database that could organize hospital records, support reliable SQL analysis, and provide actionable operational and financial insights.

02

Database Design

A normalized eight-table relational structure.

The database followed third normal form to reduce redundancy, preserve data integrity, and create reliable relationships between hospital records.

Patient
Appointment
Doctor
Department
Diagnosis
Appointment Diagnosis
Billing
Insurance

03

Implementation

Built, imported, and validated in PostgreSQL.

Database Creation

  • Created PostgreSQL tables using DDL statements
  • Defined primary and foreign keys
  • Added data types and required-field constraints
  • Created tables in the correct dependency order

Data Import and Validation

  • Imported CSV data using PostgreSQL COPY commands
  • Confirmed row counts and data types
  • Tested relational integrity
  • Validated joins across operational tables

04

SQL Analysis

Testing relationships between hospital records.

This query joins appointment records with patient and doctor information to confirm that the relational connections work correctly. 

SELECT
    a.appointment_id,
    a.appointment_datetime,
    p.first_name AS patient,
    d.first_name AS doctor
FROM appointment AS a
JOIN patient AS p
    ON a.patient_id = p.patient_id
JOIN doctor AS d
    ON a.doctor_id = d.doctor_id
LIMIT 10;

05

Analytics

Operational and financial questions explored.

Appointment Volume

Compared appointment activity across departments to identify areas with the highest service demand.

No-Show Rate

Evaluated monthly no-show patterns to identify potential scheduling and patient communication issues.

Doctor Workload

Compared appointment counts by provider to identify workload imbalances.

Diagnosis Frequency

Identified frequently recorded conditions to support staffing and resource planning.

Revenue and Payments

Compared charges with payments to evaluate financial recovery.

Visit Duration

Reviewed average appointment duration by visit status.

06

Findings

Key insights identified through the analysis.

High-Demand Departments

Cardiology and Radiology showed the highest appointment volumes, suggesting stronger demand for cardiovascular and imaging services.

Uneven Provider Workload

Some doctors handled significantly more appointments than others, indicating potential workload imbalance.

Common Diagnoses

Hypertension, asthma, flu, and COVID-19 appeared frequently, providing useful information for staffing and resource planning.

Financial Recovery Gaps

Some departments collected less relative to charges, suggesting opportunities to improve insurance verification and billing follow-up.

07

Recommendations

Actions supported by the data.

Reduce No-Show Rates

Introduce appointment reminders through email or text messaging and make rescheduling easier.

Balance Provider Workload

Adjust scheduling templates and redirect patients toward underutilized providers.

Improve Financial Recovery

Investigate departments with lower payment-to-charge ratios and strengthen insurance verification and billing follow-up.

Use Diagnosis Data for Planning

Apply diagnosis frequency data to staffing, equipment, and preventive-care planning.

Maintain Operational Reporting

Continue regular dashboard reviews and automate database extraction where possible.
